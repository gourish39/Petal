# Petal's Adventures: Panel Review P2, Dubai/GCC Cultural Consultant

**Scope:** `series_bible.md`, `ep01_script.md` to `ep05_script.md`
**Remit:** authenticity, cultural sensitivity (dogs, cats and public space in the UAE, names and Arabic words, festive imagery) and the local details Dubai residents will recognise and share.
**Threshold:** 8 on every criterion. **Result: none of the 5 episodes passes as written.** The writing is warm and well built. The failures are mostly *place* failures: the scripts treat Dubai as a backdrop rather than as somewhere people live, and two scenes put a dog where many UAE viewers will react with "that would never be allowed" before they get to "aww".

---

## 0. Series-level findings (read first)

### 0.1 Dogs in UAE public space: the single biggest risk
- Many UAE residents are fond of dogs. Many others, including a large share of the Emirati and wider Muslim audience, keep their distance from them, and a common religious view treats dog saliva as ritually impure. Cats are the opposite: they are loved, they are everywhere, and many people associate them with the Prophet's affection for cats. **Your cat characters are the most culturally safe assets in the show. Your dog-in-food and dog-in-public-walkway beats are the least safe.**
- Dogs are banned or tightly restricted in most public parks, on public beaches and on several busy community walkways. Dubai rules require dogs to be leashed in public, and owners clean up after them. Dog access to the Marina/JBR waterfront is a recurring local argument. A dog **off-leash, racing and sleeping belly-up in the middle of Marina Walk** (Ep1) and a dog **nose-deep in open spice sacks and sneezing on the stock** (Ep2) will draw comment-section fights from residents on both sides. That hurts shareability with exactly the local audience you want.
- **Series rule to add to the bible:** in public scenes (Marina, souk), Petal and Biscuit wear harnesses with a leash that visibly runs off-frame to the human; you already have "the human is a hand", so this costs nothing. No dog touches food that is for sale. Off-leash freedom is kept for private spaces (villa, rooftop) and the desert.

### 0.2 Sound design: no adhan, ever
The souk (Ep2) and skyline (Ep5) ambiences are where stock "Middle East" libraries slip in the call to prayer. **Never use the adhan as ambience or comedy timing** in a pet cartoon. Audit every stock ambience track. Oud, darbuka and pizzicato are fine and well chosen.

### 0.3 Symbols
- **Sultana's gold crescent charm.** The crescent is read as an Islamic symbol. Putting it on the vain character who gets soaked and humiliated is an avoidable risk. **Swap it for a single gold-set pearl.** The Gulf's pearling heritage is a point of national pride, it is a lovely local nod, and it still reads as "royal".
- **Ep5 "festive string lanterns".** If the animators reach for *fanous* (Ramadan lanterns) or crescent-and-star bunting, a dog birthday party starts to borrow Ramadan or Eid imagery. Specify **warm fairy lights and plain pierced-brass Arabesque lanterns**, with no crescents and no "Ramadan Kareem" décor.
- **UAE flag.** Do not put the flag, or the flag's colours as a costume, on any animal. If you post around Eid Al Etihad (2 Dec), use fireworks over the skyline and keep the flag off the cast.
- **Burj silhouette.** The bible's "Burj-style" wording is correct. Do **not** write "Happy 15 Petal" on the tower's LED façade, even though it is tempting: it implies a real-world endorsement.

### 0.4 Names check

| Name | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Jamal** (camel toy) | **Keep. It is your best local asset, and it is currently wasted.** | *Jamal* (جمل, "jamal") means camel; the man's name *Jamāl* (جمال) means beauty. The pun is a real Dubai-delight moment, but nothing on screen lands it. Add it once (see Ep1 or Ep5 fixes). It is not a sacred name, there is no offence, and the toy is affectionate. |
| **Captain Kebab** | **Rename recommended (not a blocker).** | It is harmless in itself, but it reads generic or Turkish rather than Dubai. In some corners of the internet "kebab" is part of an anti-Muslim meme ("remove kebab"), so **never pair it with hashtags or puns** like #kebab or #removekebab. **Better: "Captain Karak".** Karak chai is *the* Dubai street drink, its caramel-ginger colour **matches his ginger-tabby coat**, and **karak is brewed with cardamom, which is his Ep2 gift.** That is three wins in one name. The second choice is "Captain Shawarma". |
| **Sultana** | Keep. | It is Arabic and Turkish for a female sovereign and is also a raisin in English, so it reads as playful. Keep her a fairy-tale diva and nothing that could look like satire of real rulers (see the "Kneel." note in Ep3). |
| **Noor** | Keep. It is excellent. | *Noor* (نور) means "light", and it is a very common, warm name. Make it pay off: in Ep4 Petal's tail becomes a light, and Noor is the one who sees it first. |
| **Biscuit / Mochi / Petal** | Keep. | They are neutral. Mochi's influencer persona is very Dubai. |
| **Biscuit as a Saluki** | **Keep and exploit.** | The Saluki (*saluqi*) is the prized, heritage desert hound of the Gulf, valued by Emiratis in a way most dogs are not, and Saluki racing is a real heritage-festival sport. **"A Saluki lost a race to a 15-year-old"** is a hook UAE viewers will instantly get. None of the episodes uses it yet. |
| **"Gotcha Day"** | Keep, but explain it once. | It is a Western rescue term and much of the UAE audience won't know it. Ep5 needs a 1-line gloss (see the fixes). |

### 0.5 Continuity errors that also affect clarity on mute
- **Ep3: "12 years of garden intel" / "12 years. Same schedule."** Petal was found in 2011 and is 15. Either change it to **15** or write in that she moved to the villa in 2014. As written, viewers who count will say "wait, I thought she was 15".
- **"15 Smells for 15 Years" vs 🎁 1/5–5/5 vs "Smell #15: home".** Only 4 smells plus Jamal are collected, which makes 5. A mute viewer cannot reconcile "#15" with "5/5". The fix is in Ep5.

### 0.6 Seasonality opportunity (the review date is Nov 2026)
- **Dubai Fitness Challenge** (normally a 30-day challenge from late Oct to late Nov) fits Ep1's race *exactly*. Check the 2026 dates before you lean on it.
- **Winter is dog season.** Desert walks at Al Qudra and outdoor life only work Oct–Apr, so this release window is right. Do not release the desert or Marina episodes in July.
- **Dubai Shopping Festival** fireworks (mid-Dec to Jan) can justify fireworks over the Ep5 rooftop skyline. They are culturally neutral and very local.
